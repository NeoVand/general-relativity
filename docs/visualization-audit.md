# Visualization audit

The later [collection-wide aesthetic review](aesthetic-review.md) revisits every authored visualization for composition, hierarchy and elegance, records remaining weaknesses, and documents the subsequent figure and scene refinements. The account below records the earlier revision.

The weakest part of the original system was its composition. A large scene, a separate instruction bar, a full-width browser slider, floating listening buttons, an equation, an explanatory note, and then another drawing competed to introduce the same idea. The result was more interface than understanding. The controls also suggested that these were unrelated widgets rather than illustrations in one book.

The revision uses one illustration container. **Explore** contains the spatial model; **Diagram** contains the companion drawing and its own caption. These are complementary explanations, not necessarily literal projections of the same geometry. The alternate diagram uses its own caption; the spatial model’s equation and caveat do not remain underneath it as though they described the same representation. The diagram remains a usable fallback when WebGL is unavailable. Its original figure anchor and enlargement control remain available. One reading passage represents the paired illustration, preventing the former automatic narration duplication.

The scene's top edge holds the view switch, Listen, Explain, and reset. Orbit remains a direct manipulation, with arrow-key rotation and Home to reset. Parameters use a two-pixel track, a small thumb inside a larger interaction target, an aligned label and mathematical value, and quiet endpoints. The spatial canvas is framed from its actual geometry and label anchors over a sample of the parameter range. It no longer depends on one guessed zoom multiplier for many different shapes. Camera reset restores that framing. Deliberate zooming can still enlarge objects beyond the viewport.

## Quality bar for future visual work

1. State the physical or mathematical question before drawing its answer.
2. Introduce one new representation at a time, with a worked reading of it.
3. Let color identify the same mathematical role across text, equations and scenes.
4. Keep all mathematical labels in LaTeX, and attach moving labels to the objects they describe.
5. Show one primary view at a time unless simultaneous comparison is the actual lesson.
6. Explain the modeled domain, approximation and suppressed dimensions where they affect interpretation.
7. Use motion to reveal a dependency, not merely to make an illustration look alive.
8. Fit the full intended model and annotation envelope on desktop and phone, at the control extremes as well as at the default.
9. Keep direct manipulation, keyboard equivalents and a recoverable default view.
10. Treat narration as an alternative way through the same lesson, with one concise script per illustration.

## Scope and limits

This revision rebuilds the illustration shell, control treatment, complementary-view behavior, several moving annotations and camera framing. It does not claim to replace the physical models with research simulators or to implement every proposed deeper lesson in the table. The retained models are analytic demonstrations: a Schwarzschild exterior rain frame, Euclidean covector pairing, a 2+1-dimensional light cone, spherical holonomy, an early-time Newtonian tidal cloud, one Schwarzschild spatial embedding, a linearized plus-polarized wave, a selected spatially flat FLRW slice, and schematic lapse/shift.

## Audit of all 40 vector figures

All forty light-theme figures were inspected visually using their full-resolution QA captures, assembled into contact sheets for comparisons of typography, composition and density. No new concrete label collision or cropped annotation was apparent in those captures. The much larger issue is **density at phone width**: a two-column drawing that is clear at its 1000-unit design width can turn into tiny text when scaled into a 350-pixel reading column. The enlargement control preserves access, but a future responsive rebuild should place diagram labels and explanations in HTML and let panels stack. Simply adding more 3D would not solve this.

“Keep” means the visual form is appropriate; “tighten” means its explanation or emphasis should improve; “rebuild” means its teaching sequence or representation needs more substantial work. These are audit findings and proposals, not claims that all forty diagrams were redesigned in this revision.

## Verification of this revision

- `npm run test:experience`: passed all 36 combinations of nine scenes, two viewport sizes and two themes, including model endpoints, maximum-value label clearance, camera keyboard controls, paired-view visibility/accessibility, canonical figure links, WebGL fallback, Earth animation suspension and reduced motion.
- `npm run test:figures`: passed all 40 SVGs in both themes; no clipped text, text collisions, strokes crossing labels, or opaque shapes painted over labels.
- Visual review caught an over-conservative camera fit that the automated checks did not: the initial bounding-box approach made the sphere and embedding too small. Actual projected geometry now supplies the fit. A later endpoint check caught the long tidal eigenvalue label at phone width; framing now includes the rendered label width.
- Full-resolution scene captures, contact-sheet inspection of all forty vector figures, and a normal-scroll phone inspection were used in addition to geometric assertions. The remaining deeper teaching and responsive-diagram limitations are listed above rather than represented as completed work.
