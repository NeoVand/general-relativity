# Field energy: first-use preparation

Section 11.6 previously introduced the electromagnetic tensor without first defining electric charge, the electric and magnetic fields, or their cross product. It also claimed a preceding example had defined the Poynting vector when that definition was missing.

The revised section starts from what the fields do to a test charge. It states the Lorentz force, explains the cross product geometrically and in Cartesian components, supplies SI units, and distinguishes electric work from the magnetic change of momentum direction. Only then does it collect the six field components into the antisymmetric tensor. Substitution into the covariant force law checks the signs and factors of c for the book's signature. Vacuum constants and the energy-flux vector are defined before use. A plane-wave example connects equal electric/magnetic energy contributions, energy flux at c, and forward momentum; parallel fields provide a counterexample to the idea that stored field energy must flow.

The field-action formulas remain an explicitly identified preview of Chapter 13. This repair is local preparation, not a complete course in Maxwell's equations. A corresponding visual field-energy experiment and the remaining chapter-by-chapter review are still open.
